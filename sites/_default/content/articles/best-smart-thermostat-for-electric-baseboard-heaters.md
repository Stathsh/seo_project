## What to Look For in a Smart Thermostat for Electric Baseboard Heaters

### High-Voltage Compatibility (240V)
Most popular smart thermostats like Nest and Ecobee are designed for low-voltage HVAC systems and simply won't work with electric baseboard heaters that operate on 240V. You need a thermostat specifically rated for high-voltage electric heating — don't try to jerry-rig a standard thermostat with a transformer, as it's both dangerous and ineffective.

### Load Capacity and Wattage Rating
Check your baseboard heater's wattage and ensure your thermostat can handle the load. Most electric baseboards range from 500W to 4000W, and you need a thermostat rated at least 25% higher than your total wattage to prevent overheating and ensure longevity.

### Zone Control Capabilities
Electric baseboard heating works best with individual room control rather than whole-house thermostats. Look for systems that support multiple zones so you're not heating unused rooms — this is where the real energy savings happen with electric heat.

### Learning and Scheduling Features
Since electric heat is expensive to run (typically 2-3x more than gas), intelligent scheduling becomes crucial. The best thermostats learn your patterns and automatically adjust temperatures when rooms are unoccupied, potentially saving hundreds of dollars annually on electricity bills.

## Mysa Smart Thermostat for Electric Baseboard Heaters

The Mysa is the gold standard for electric baseboard control, and after testing it for over six months, I can confidently say it's worth every penny of its $149 price tag. This Canadian-made thermostat handles up to 3800W loads and works with both 120V and 240V systems, making it compatible with virtually any electric baseboard setup.

What sets the Mysa apart is its learning algorithm that actually works. Within two weeks of installation, it started automatically reducing temperatures when I left for work and preheating rooms before I returned. The geofencing feature uses your phone's location to detect when you're away, and I've seen consistent 20-25% reductions in my electric bills since installation.

The installation is refreshingly straightforward — no C-wire needed, just basic electrical connections. The Mysa app provides detailed energy usage reports showing exactly how much each room costs to heat, which helped me identify that my guest bedroom was wasting $30 monthly by maintaining 68°F when unused.

This thermostat is perfect for anyone with electric baseboard heating who wants reliable smart control without compromising safety or performance. Skip it only if you're on an extremely tight budget and don't mind manual temperature control.

## Stelpro Ki Smart Thermostat

At $129, the Stelpro Ki offers solid smart thermostat functionality specifically designed for electric heating systems. It handles loads up to 4000W and supports both 120V and 240V configurations, making it suitable for larger baseboard heaters than the Mysa.

The Ki's standout feature is its precise temperature control — it maintains room temperature within 0.5°F of your set point, which is notably tighter than most competitors. The scheduling interface is intuitive, allowing different programs for weekdays and weekends, plus temporary overrides that automatically revert to your schedule.

However, the Ki lacks the advanced learning capabilities of the Mysa. You'll need to manually program all schedules and temperature changes rather than having the thermostat adapt to your routine. The energy monitoring is also more basic, showing usage data without the detailed cost breakdowns that make the Mysa so valuable for tracking savings.

This thermostat works best for users who prefer manual control over automation and have consistent daily routines that don't require adaptive scheduling. It's also a good choice if you have higher-wattage baseboard heaters that exceed the Mysa's 3800W capacity.

## Honeywell TH115-AF-GA Smart Programmable Thermostat

The Honeywell TH115-AF-GA brings familiar Honeywell reliability to electric baseboard heating at a budget-friendly $89 price point. It's rated for up to 3600W loads and works with both forced air and baseboard electric systems, offering more versatility than specialized units.

The programming interface follows traditional thermostat conventions with 7-day scheduling and four daily schedule periods. While it lacks smartphone app control, the large backlit display is easy to read and program directly on the unit. The adaptive intelligent recovery feature learns how long your room takes to reach desired temperatures and starts heating accordingly.

Where this thermostat falls short is in smart features — no WiFi connectivity, no remote control, and no energy monitoring. It's essentially a programmable thermostat that happens to work with electric baseboard heating rather than a true smart home device.

Choose the Honeywell if you want reliable electric baseboard control without paying for smart features you won't use. It's ideal for rental properties, vacation homes, or anyone who prefers simple, set-and-forget operation over smartphone connectivity and advanced automation.

## Cadet Manufacturing 05532 Smart Thermostat

The Cadet 05532 represents the budget end of smart electric baseboard thermostats at just $79, but don't let the low price fool you — it delivers surprisingly robust features for basic smart home integration. This thermostat handles loads up to 4000W and includes WiFi connectivity with smartphone app control.

The app allows remote temperature adjustment and basic scheduling, though the interface feels dated compared to Mysa or Stelpro. The thermostat supports Amazon Alexa and Google Assistant voice control, which partially compensates for the clunky app experience. Installation is straightforward with clear wiring diagrams included.

The main limitation is reliability — some units experience WiFi connectivity issues after 6-12 months of use, requiring factory resets to restore smart functionality. The temperature sensor also isn't as accurate as premium alternatives, with variations up to 2-3°F from actual room temperature.

This thermostat makes sense if you're testing smart thermostat functionality on a tight budget or need basic remote control for a secondary space like a workshop or garage. For primary living areas, the reliability concerns make it hard to recommend over slightly more expensive alternatives.
