The April 2024 solar eclipse can cause power grid fluctuations as solar panels across the path of totality suddenly lose and regain power generation. To protect your smart home devices, unplug non-essential electronics, ensure critical devices have battery backup, and configure your smart home hub to automatically save device states before the eclipse begins.

## Understanding Solar Eclipse Power Grid Impact

Solar eclipses create unique challenges for modern power grids, especially in areas with high solar panel adoption. As the moon blocks sunlight, thousands of solar installations simultaneously reduce their output, forcing utilities to rapidly switch to backup power sources. When the eclipse ends, this process reverses just as quickly, creating voltage spikes and frequency variations that can stress electronic equipment.

Your smart home devices are particularly vulnerable because they rely on stable power and consistent network connectivity. Unlike traditional appliances that simply turn on and off, smart devices maintain constant communication with cloud servers, local hubs, and each other. Power fluctuations can corrupt firmware, reset configurations, or damage sensitive components like Wi-Fi radios and microprocessors.

The April 2024 eclipse will be especially challenging because it occurs during peak solar generation hours and crosses major metropolitan areas with extensive smart grid infrastructure. Texas, Ohio, and New York—all states in the path of totality—have invested heavily in both solar power and smart home adoption, creating the perfect storm for potential disruptions.

## Pre-Eclipse Device Inventory and Prioritization

Start by creating a comprehensive list of all your smart home devices, categorizing them by importance and power requirements. Critical devices include security cameras, smart locks, and medical monitoring equipment that shouldn't lose power or connectivity. Secondary devices like smart speakers, lighting controls, and entertainment systems can safely be unplugged without affecting home security or safety.

Pay special attention to devices with internal batteries versus those requiring constant power. Smart thermostats, for instance, usually have backup batteries that maintain basic functionality during brief outages, while smart displays and always-on security cameras need uninterrupted power to function properly. Document which devices are hardwired versus plugged in—hardwired smoke detectors and security panels are harder to protect but more critical to maintain.

Consider your network infrastructure as the backbone of your smart home. Your router, modem, and any mesh network nodes should be your highest priority for power protection. Without stable internet connectivity, even battery-powered smart devices lose much of their functionality and can't receive important updates or commands during the eclipse period.

## Power Protection Strategies for Eclipse Day

The most effective protection strategy combines selective unplugging with battery backup systems. Three hours before the eclipse begins in your area, unplug all non-essential smart devices including smart TVs, voice assistants, smart plugs controlling lamps, and any devices you can easily reconfigure later. This eliminates the risk of damage from power surges while reducing the overall load on your electrical system.

For critical devices, invest in uninterruptible power supplies (UPS) units rated for your specific equipment. A good UPS doesn't just provide backup power—it also filters incoming electricity and protects against surges. Choose UPS units with sine wave output rather than modified sine wave, as smart devices with sensitive electronics perform better with clean power. Size your UPS to provide at least 2-3 hours of runtime for essential devices.

Whole-house surge protectors offer another layer of defense, though they're most effective when combined with point-of-use protection. If you don't already have one installed, consider having an electrician add a whole-house surge protector before the eclipse. These devices clamp voltage spikes at your electrical panel before they can reach your smart home equipment throughout the house.

## Network and Hub Configuration Best Practices

Configure your smart home hub to automatically save device states and settings at least 24 hours before the eclipse. Most hubs like SmartThings, Hubitat, and Home Assistant have backup features, but you need to manually initiate the process. Create a complete backup that includes device pairings, automation rules, and custom configurations—restoring from backup is much faster than recreating everything manually.

Update all device firmware at least one week before the eclipse, not during eclipse week. Power fluctuations during firmware updates can permanently damage devices or leave them in an unusable state. Check your hub's event logs and clear any pending updates or error messages that might complicate recovery efforts if devices need to reconnect after power issues.

Consider temporarily simplifying your automation rules for eclipse day. Complex automations with multiple triggers and conditions are more likely to behave unpredictably if devices briefly lose connectivity or reset. Disable non-essential automations and keep only critical ones like security alerts and emergency lighting active until normal grid conditions resume.

## Post-Eclipse Recovery and Testing Protocol

Once power conditions stabilize (typically 2-3 hours after totality ends), begin reconnecting devices systematically rather than plugging everything in simultaneously. Start with your network equipment—router, modem, and any mesh nodes—and wait for full connectivity before proceeding to your smart home hub. This staged approach prevents network congestion and helps identify any devices that may have been damaged.

Test critical functions immediately: door locks should respond to codes and smartphone commands, security cameras should stream properly, and smart thermostats should maintain temperature settings. If any device fails to reconnect or behaves erratically, power cycle it completely (unplug for 30 seconds) before assuming it's damaged. Many smart devices simply need a fresh connection attempt after power anomalies.

Document any devices that required reconfiguration or replacement—this information helps you prepare better for future events and may be relevant for insurance claims if expensive equipment was damaged. Update your device inventory with any lessons learned and consider whether additional protection measures would be worthwhile for similar events.
