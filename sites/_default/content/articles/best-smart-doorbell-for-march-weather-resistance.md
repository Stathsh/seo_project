## What to Look For in a Weather-Resistant Smart Doorbell

### IP Rating and Temperature Range
The IP (Ingress Protection) rating tells you exactly how well your doorbell handles dust and water. Look for at least IP65 rating for serious weather protection — IP6X means complete dust protection, while IPX5 handles water jets from any direction. Pay attention to operating temperature ranges too; cheap doorbells often fail when temperatures drop below 20°F or climb above 120°F.

### Power Source and Battery Performance
Hardwired doorbells typically outperform battery models in extreme weather since cold temperatures can drain lithium batteries faster than expected. If you go battery-powered, look for models with removable batteries or dual-battery systems. Some newer models offer solar charging panels as backup power sources.

### Build Quality and Mounting System
Metal construction beats plastic every time for weather resistance. Check that mounting hardware includes weatherproof gaskets and corrosion-resistant screws. The best weather-resistant doorbells use marine-grade materials and have sealed button mechanisms that won't stick or fail when exposed to moisture and temperature fluctuations.

### Connectivity Reliability
Weather can interfere with wireless signals, so dual-band Wi-Fi (2.4GHz and 5GHz) gives you better connection stability. Some premium models include cellular backup or mesh network capabilities. Look for doorbells with local storage options since cloud connectivity might be spotty during severe weather.
