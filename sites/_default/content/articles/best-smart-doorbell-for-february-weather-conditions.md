## What to Look For in a February Weather Doorbell

When shopping for a smart doorbell that can handle February's harsh conditions, **power source** should be your first consideration. Battery-powered doorbells lose 20-40% of their capacity in sub-freezing temperatures, leading to frequent dead batteries right when you need security most. Hardwired models maintain consistent power and performance regardless of temperature.

**Motion detection technology** becomes critical when snow and ice are involved. Standard PIR (passive infrared) sensors can be fooled by temperature variations and snow accumulation, while radar-based detection cuts through weather interference. Look for doorbells with dual detection methods that combine multiple technologies.

**Field of view width** matters more in winter than other seasons. You'll often need to mount doorbells at different angles to avoid snow accumulation or ice damage, and a wider viewing angle ensures you still capture important activity like package deliveries. Aim for at least 140° horizontal coverage.

**Operating temperature range** separates the serious contenders from fair-weather devices. Many budget doorbells stop working reliably below 32°F, but quality models should function down to -5°F or lower. Check the manufacturer's specified operating range, not just their storage temperature specs.
