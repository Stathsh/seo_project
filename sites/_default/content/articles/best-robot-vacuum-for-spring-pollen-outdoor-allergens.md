## What to Look For in a Robot Vacuum for Pollen and Allergens

### True HEPA Filtration
Not all "HEPA-style" filters are created equal. Look for vacuums with genuine HEPA filters that meet the strict 99.97% efficiency standard for 0.3-micron particles. Pollen grains typically range from 10-100 microns, but the finest particles and pollen fragments that trigger the worst allergic reactions are much smaller. A true HEPA filter will capture these invisible irritants that lesser filters miss.

### High Suction Power and Multiple Cleaning Passes
Pollen doesn't just sit on surfaces—it embeds in carpet fibers and settles into cracks between floorboards. Look for models with at least 2000Pa of suction power and multi-pass cleaning patterns. The best allergen-fighting robots make 2-3 passes over each area to ensure deep extraction of embedded particles.

### Smart Navigation to Avoid Cross-Contamination
Basic bump-and-go robots can actually spread pollen around by dragging their brushes from dirty areas to clean ones. Advanced mapping and navigation prevent this cross-contamination by following efficient, methodical cleaning patterns and avoiding obstacles that could harbor allergens.

### Self-Emptying Base Station
Manual dustbin emptying defeats the purpose of allergen control—you'll release captured pollen right back into the air. Self-emptying bases with sealed bags keep allergens locked away for weeks, and many feature additional HEPA filtration in the base itself.
