## What to Look For in a Smart Thermostat for Mini Splits

### IR Signal Compatibility and Range
Unlike central HVAC systems that use simple on/off signals, mini splits communicate through complex infrared codes that vary dramatically between manufacturers. Look for controllers with extensive IR databases — the best options support 3,000+ mini split models from brands like Mitsubishi, Daikin, LG, and Friedrich. Signal strength matters too; cheaper units often fail beyond 6 feet, while premium models maintain reliable communication up to 25 feet even with obstacles.

### Multi-Zone Management Capabilities  
Most homes with mini splits have multiple indoor units serving different zones. Your smart thermostat solution should handle independent scheduling, temperature targets, and mode settings for each zone without requiring separate apps or accounts. Advanced models offer load balancing to optimize energy usage across zones and prevent simultaneous startup spikes.

### Temperature Sensing Accuracy
Mini splits cycle differently than central systems, making precise temperature measurement critical for comfort and efficiency. Basic IR controllers rely solely on the unit's built-in sensors, which can be poorly positioned. Premium options include dedicated room sensors or use the controller's onboard sensors, providing ±0.5°F accuracy compared to ±2-3°F for budget alternatives.

### Smart Home Integration Depth
Beyond basic voice commands, look for native integrations that expose individual zone controls, energy monitoring, and advanced scheduling through platforms like SmartThings, Home Assistant, or Apple HomeKit. The best controllers offer local API access for custom automations and don't rely entirely on cloud connectivity for core functions.
