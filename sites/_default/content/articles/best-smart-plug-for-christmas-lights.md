## What to Look For in a Smart Plug for Christmas Lights

**Load capacity matters more than you think.** Most people underestimate how much power their Christmas light displays actually draw. A typical 100-count LED string uses about 5-7 watts, but when you're daisy-chaining multiple strings or mixing in older incandescent bulbs, you can easily hit 10-15 amps. Look for smart plugs rated for at least 15 amps to handle heavy-duty displays without tripping breakers or overheating.

**Weather resistance is non-negotiable for outdoor setups.** Indoor-rated plugs will fail within weeks when exposed to moisture, temperature swings, and UV rays. For outdoor Christmas lights, you need plugs with IP44 or higher weather ratings, proper gasket sealing, and housings designed to shed water effectively.

**Scheduling flexibility separates good from great.** Basic on/off timers work fine, but the best Christmas light smart plugs offer sunrise/sunset automation, multiple daily schedules, and the ability to sync with other smart home devices. During the holidays, you want your lights turning on automatically as it gets dark, not at a fixed time that becomes obsolete as daylight hours change.

**Physical design impacts real-world usability.** Bulky smart plugs that block both outlets in a duplex receptacle are frustrating when you're trying to power multiple light strings. The best options maintain a compact profile or offer multiple controlled outlets in a single unit.
