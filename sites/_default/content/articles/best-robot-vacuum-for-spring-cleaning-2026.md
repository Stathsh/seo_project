## What to Look For in a Spring Cleaning Robot Vacuum

**Self-Emptying Capability is Non-Negotiable**
Spring cleaning generates 3-4x more debris than regular maintenance cleaning. You need a base that can handle at least 30 days of dirt without attention, preferably 60+ days. Look for bases with sealed bags (not bagless systems) to prevent allergens from escaping back into your air.

**Advanced Dirt Detection Technology**
The best spring cleaning robots use optical sensors or acoustic detection to identify high-traffic areas that accumulated extra grime over winter. They'll automatically make multiple passes over these spots instead of treating your whole house uniformly. Basic models with only bump sensors won't cut it.

**Strong Suction with Variable Power**
You need at least 2,500 Pa of suction to pull embedded dirt from carpets and 40+ CFM airflow to handle fine dust. More importantly, look for models that can automatically boost power when transitioning from hard floors to rugs, since spring cleaning involves tackling both surface types effectively.

**Multi-Surface Brush Systems**
Dual-brush systems with rubber and bristle rollers handle the variety of spring debris better than single-brush designs. The rubber roller grabs pet hair and larger particles while bristles agitate ground-in dirt. Anti-tangle technology becomes crucial when dealing with the increased hair shedding that comes with warmer weather.
